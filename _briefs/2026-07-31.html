Correcting our own account of the Microsoft useful-life change. Hood said the fifteen-to-twenty-five-year extension is effective at the start of FY27, not calendar 2026, and covers data centres and office buildings, not servers. The mechanism is a lease reclassification: a longer assumed life pushes certain leases from finance treatment, which counts in reported capex, to operating treatment, which does not. Hood: "Finance leases are included in capital expenditures, while operating leases are not," and "Outside of this useful life impact, our calendar year 2026 CapEx investment expectations remain unchanged." The ~$15bn is a derived delta — $190bn less the $175bn she gave — computed by reporters, not spoken by Microsoft. Actual spend is rising: FQ4 capex including finance leases was roughly $41bn, up about 70 per cent, with FQ1 FY27 guided above $50bn.

And no hyperscaler followed. Amazon disclosed no useful-life change; Jassy's thirty-year-life remark is a return-on-capital defence, not an accounting election. Amazon in fact moved the other way in January 2025, shortening server life from six years to five for a subset. The 30 July ledger row asked whether the depreciation election would spread; on two prints, it did not. Agentic systems ·longIt is a two-lab pattern now, and both labs found it by going looking

Read against OpenAI's incident this changes the shape. Policy & regulation ·mediumFive regulatory items, four misdescribed

Gigafactories. Not €10bn committed to seven sites in five countries. EuroHPC opened a tender for up to seven on 30 July, proposals due 12 November, selection early 2027. The ~€30bn headline is ~€10bn public plus ~€20bn hoped-for private, and the public tranche is EU plus member states — the Commission can commit roughly €1bn now, the rest contingent on an unnegotiated 2028–2035 MFF. Ten countries have signalled interest, including France, which the observatory's list omits. The 2025 informal call drew 76 expressions of interest across 16 member states requesting at least three million GPUs; against seven awards, the Commission's problem is that it must disappoint most of the continent.

DSA. No designation has occurred; Bloomberg reported on 29 July it could come "as soon as August," and the Commission's spokesperson said only that it is "definitely possible." The category is also wrong — ChatGPT's search function would be a VLOSE, a different Article 33 class. CMA. The 29 July case is consumer protection, into whether Microsoft 365 customers "were given key information about the plans and the difference in cost" before the Copilot price rise — not AI bundling, which belongs to the separate Strategic Market Status case of 14 May. The understated part is procedural: the CMA can now find breach itself, at the same 10-per-cent-of-turnover maximum. Brazil. 16 August is a filing deadline for platform compliance plans; the labelling duty was adopted 2 March 2026 and already binds, and it also bans outright synthetic sexual content and political violence against women and freezes new synthetic candidate content 72 hours before the vote — a genuine novelty the "disclosure" framing understates.

FCC. DA 26-786 is both broader and narrower than reported. Broader: all ground-based mobile robots over 4.4 pounds meeting sensor, connectivity and autonomy criteria, not only humanoids and quadrupeds — and it also adds foreign-produced power inverters, the larger economic action, omitted entirely. Narrower: it bars equipment authorisation for new models, not imports, carving out previously authorised models, installed equipment, connected and rail vehicles, UAS, underwater vehicles, FDA-classified medical devices, fixed industrial arms, and DoW/DHS conditional approval. It names no Chinese company, defining "foreign-produced" by a Buy American content test. "Washington mirrored this move" is an ordering error — an action of this complexity was not drafted in the day after the K3 release.

Congress. The AI Ads Act was reintroduced 27 July by Schiff and Khanna; the sponsor's own posted text is headed "S. __". No number exists, nor for the AI Kill Switch Act or the FRONTIER Act of 23 July. The FRONTIER Act's real significance is preemption — it "creates a uniform national standard… to prevent a patchwork of state regulations" — landing the fortnight California takes up thirty-plus AI bills. And a pattern nobody names: the Kill Switch Act would vest authority in the DHS Secretary, and EO 14409 §3 makes Treasury, War-through-NSA and DHS-through-CISA the acting principals with Commerce-through-NIST merely consulted. Frontier-AI authority is migrating from the standards track to the security agencies.

Technical research ·mediumThe distillation negative survives a fresh sweep, with three wording fixes

The standing negative — no method demonstrated on two deployed frontier models, none covering reward-model-mediated or RL-based distillation — holds against a nine-query sweep of search-indexed arXiv through 31 July across eleven candidate papers. Scope limit, stated plainly: the arXiv API and full-text search UI both returned PROVENANCE_REQUIRED, so the negative rests on search indexing, which lags for preprints under ten days old.

Three corrections, all understating a rival result. Shadow-MoE (2510.16968) reports 100 per cent accuracy in the fully black-box setting, not merely the >94 per cent headline. It is not strictly MoE-only — shadow proxies exist precisely because "many models are dense or API-limited" — so the honest formulation is that dense coverage is a constructed workaround the authors list as future work. And the "OLMoE checkpoints" ground-truth detail did not re-confirm; a fetch surfaced DeepSeek-R1 as teacher. Verify before that sentence appears in print. Conversely 2510.02302 was demonstrated on CIFAR-10, ImageNet and text-to-image models — not on language models at all — which makes the negative stronger than we have been claiming.

On security: MIT Technology Review's 30 July piece says flatly that "it is impossible to make large language models fully secure against hacks because of a fundamental flaw in how they work." The underlying research — "Prompt Injection as Role Confusion," Ye, Cui and Hadfield-Menell, ICML 2026 poster — makes a conditional claim: "Unless LLMs achieve genuine role perception, we think injection defense will remain a perpetual whack-a-mole game." Models infer roles from style rather than structural tags. Note the scope: this explains why inputs subvert a model, not why a sandbox leaked. Attaching it to the OpenAI escape is a category error.

Labour & workforce ·mediumThe wage channel finally has a number, and it points both ways

On 30 July Apollo published a white paper by Sania Edlich and Torsten Sløk finding that high-AI-exposure occupations saw an average 6.7 per cent decline in real wage growth after 2023 — service workers −24.3, bottom-quartile earners −10.7, computer programmers −6.1 — across 321 occupations on BLS data for 2022–2024, while aggregate employment effects were not "detectable." This is the first thing to report a wage effect while finding no employment effect, which is the discriminating test this brief has been asking for.

Two things in the same breath. It is an asset manager's white paper, not peer-reviewed, and its exposure variable is Anthropic's own Economic Index — using an AI company's telemetry to measure AI's labour harm is a circularity a hostile reader finds in thirty seconds. And the Dallas Fed found the opposite sign in February: employment in AI-exposed sectors down about 1 per cent, computer systems design down 5, but wages in computer systems design up 16.7 per cent against 7.5 nationally. Scott Davis's mechanism — AI substitutes for codified knowledge, complements tacit — reconciles Apollo's bottom-quartile figure and contradicts its programmer figure. The channel is now contested rather than null, which is a better position than Wednesday's.

The op-ed hook

"The Best Quarter In History Was A Mark On A Company Nobody Can Sell." Amazon reported $62.6bn of net income against $27.5bn of operating income and told you in the release that $53.4bn of the difference is non-operating income "primarily from our investments in Anthropic." Microsoft booked $3.2bn on Anthropic and $480m on OpenAI. Neither has sold a share. Both sell cloud compute to the labs they are marking, and Anthropic's Azure commitment was $30bn at the time Microsoft's $5bn went in. The piece writes as an auditability argument rather than a fraud argument, which is what makes it publishable: nothing here is concealed. Every figure is in a filing. The question is who is licensed to check the valuation of a private company whose price is set by the firms booking gains on it — and what a reported earnings number means when its largest component is a number the reporter chose.

The strongest case against today's reading

The version that should sting. You have spent this brief establishing that the observatory got twenty things wrong, and every one is a detail — a date, a category, a sign, an adjective. Not one changes what a reader should do. It said the breach became a governance argument, and it did. It said Brussels is funding what it regulates, and it is. It said a free frontier model complicates every reading of China as merely repricing, and it does. It was wrong about which court, which category, which quarter, which model — and right about every structural claim it made. You were right about which quarter and have not made a structural claim in seven days.

The uncomfortable extension is that your own instrument shows this. Twenty-five instances of F10 — the adjective riding on a verified noun — against one F12. You catch modifiers. You do not catch arguments. The reason is in the method: verification is a per-claim operation, so it can only find per-claim errors. A framing wrong at the level of the whole cannot be falsified by any subagent, because there is no URL for it. An instrument that finds twenty-five attributive errors and zero framing errors is not measuring framing. It is reporting that framing is fine, which is a different statement and an unearned one.

The decision it forces. Either the error log's publishable finding is about calibration — machine-generated analysis states things at confidence the evidence does not license — which is defensible, measured and modest; or it is about reliability, which is what the framing keeps reaching for and which twenty-five adjective errors cannot support. Choose calibration. It is what the data shows, it survives the objection above, and it is the version Cowie can co-sign rather than defend against. Which raises Precondition 2, unmoved for seven days: talk to him. That the archive page he publishes reported four of his own issues out of existence for three days is the kind of thing a collaborator mentions and a critic prints.
