Technical research ·longThere is no test for the thing sanctions are being threatened over

This is the most consequential finding of the run and it is a negative. Bessent conditioned sanctions on distillation. Anthropic's 27 July position paper asks for "a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation operations." MOFCOM's 27 July response says the allegations "lack any actual evidence." No published method can establish retrospectively that a deployed model was distilled from another. The two nearest candidates: arXiv 2510.02302, "Knowledge Distillation Detection for Open-weights Models," requires the student's weights and API access to the candidate teacher, and reports results on CIFAR-10, ImageNet and text-to-image, not frontier LLMs; arXiv 2602.03812, "Antidistillation Fingerprinting," requires the teacher to have embedded fingerprints in its outputs before the distillation occurred. Neither supports a determination against a model already trained. Confidence HIGH on the two papers, MEDIUM on the claim that nothing better exists — this is a negative over the published literature as indexed, and a positive result would be a peer-reviewed or preprint method operating on outputs alone, post hoc.

The AISI/CAISI assessment, re-derived from both the aisi.gov.uk and nist.gov mirrors on 29 July. Published 23 July, genuinely joint. The metric is "The Last Ones" (TLO), a 32-step simulated corporate-network intrusion across four subnets and about twenty hosts, roughly twenty hours of human expert work. K3 reached step 17 on average; "the most cyber-capable U.S. models" reached 28.5. K3 completed TLO fully in one of ten attempts. On a second metric, ExploitBench, K3 scores 32 per cent against GLM-5.2's 24 per cent, but achieved arbitrary code execution on 0 of 41 samples against 20 of 41 for the leading US models. Neither document mentions EO 14409 or "covered frontier model," on three separate fetches.

Three qualifications the political use omits. US models were tested with system-level safeguards disabled. K3 received only "a selective set of cyber evaluations" for hosting reasons, with wider confidence intervals from a single benchmark. And TLO has, in the report's own words, an intentional attack path, no active defenders, no defensive tooling and no penalty for alert-triggering actions — it measures capability in an undefended range. The report makes no policy recommendation and does not mention export controls. Commerce posted it as evidence of American lead anyway, and the gap between what the document says and what it is used to say is the story.

One circulating figure to avoid: several outlets pair K3's 32 per cent ExploitBench score against "76 per cent" for US models. The primary comparator is 20/41 arbitrary code execution, about 49 per cent. The 76 is unsupported.

Policy & regulation ·longThe classified line, and two jurisdictions arriving on Sunday

EO 14409 was signed 2 June 2026 and published 5 June; the sixty-day mark is Saturday 1 August (CRS IF13268 v2, 9 July, states the date verbatim). §3 text was re-derived on 29 July from federalregister.gov doc 2026-11415 and presidency.ucsb.edu, returning byte-identical text. The agency list corrected on 27 July holds, with one word: it is the White House Chief of Staff.

Three things in §3 that summaries omit. First, the designation power is unitary: Treasury, DHS and Commerce build the process, but "such a determination shall be made by the Director of NSA," in consultation only. Second, the process is expressly "classified," and the sharing provision — "sharing such assessments with AI developers and researchers as appropriate" — sits inside the classified subsection, so disclosure is discretionary. Third, §3(c) is a savings clause with real content: "Nothing in this section shall be construed to authorize the creation of a mandatory governmental licensing, preclearance, or permitting requirement." Almost no summary carries it, and it is the strongest textual constraint in the order. What is due Saturday is a process and a design, not a designation; an empty 1 August is fully compliant.

Bill numbers: the AI Kill Switch Act and the FRONTIER Act, both introduced 23 July, remain unnumbered or unindexed as of 29 July. Companions introduced the same day are numbered — H.R. 9914, S. 5105 — which makes the absence meaningful rather than lag, though August recess is the likelier explanation than anything adversarial. Preemption's live vehicle is not the NDAA but the Great American AI Act discussion draft of 4 June, three-year, development-side, still unintroduced.

EU. The enacting terms of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 were read this run. Amended Article 57(1) confirms national sandboxes "operational by 2 August 2027," plus a new Union-level sandbox at the AI Office. Two new Article 5 prohibitions are confirmed verbatim — non-consensual intimate imagery at 5(1)(ba), CSAM at (bb) — but their 2 December 2026 application date was not located in the operative text (two fetches truncated inside the Article 1 block) and remains secondary. Article 50 applies Sunday 2 August; the penalty band is Article 99(4), €15m or 3 per cent of turnover, whichever is higher. The same Sunday, the California AI Transparency Act (SB 942 as amended by AB 853) becomes operative above one million monthly California users. Two jurisdictions, one subject, one date, and no source I read connected them.

UK, in one line: the AI Security Institute now sits in the Office for the Prime Minister and Cabinet alongside AI adoption strategy and the growth agenda — evaluator and promoter under one roof, no sponsoring department, and no taskforce membership, terms of reference or first meeting published.

The op-ed hook

Same hook, third sharpening, and today it stops being about the deadline. "Washington Has Threatened Sanctions For A Crime It Cannot Yet Detect."

The argument in one line: on 21 July the Treasury Secretary conditioned sanctions and Entity List designations on evidence of industrial-scale distillation; on 27 July Anthropic asked for a crackdown on the same conduct; and there is no published method that can establish, from a deployed model, that it was distilled from another. The two candidate literatures both require something you do not have after the fact — the student's weights plus the teacher's API, or fingerprints the teacher embedded in advance. Fable went public on 1 July and K3 launched on 16 July; fifteen days is a falsifiable timing test that nobody in government has publicly run. Meanwhile the one document the US government did produce about K3 — the AISI/CAISI assessment of 23 July — does not mention distillation, does not mention export controls, makes no recommendation, and compares a selectively-evaluated Chinese model against American models tested with their safeguards switched off.

Every source in that paragraph is primary and every one was fetched this morning: Bessent's and Kratsios's own posts as reported by CNBC and TechCrunch, Anthropic's own page, MOFCOM's own Chinese-language statement, the AISI and NIST mirrors, and two arXiv papers. There is no research left to do. The piece is 1,100 words and it is the strongest thing this machinery has produced in five days. It expires Saturday, when the EO date passes and the framing shifts. The strongest case against today's reading

Here is the version that should sting. Governments act on unmeasurable things constantly, and it is not a scandal; it is the ordinary condition of statecraft. Export controls have always run on inference about end use. Sanctions run on patterns nobody can prove to an evidentiary standard. The demand that Washington produce a forensic test for distillation before it may threaten sanctions is a category error dressed as rigour — it mistakes a policy instrument for a court. Bessent said the words "will be on the table," which is precisely what a credible threat under uncertainty sounds like, and if it deters Moonshot's counterparties from signing that $20m commercial agreement, it has worked without ever needing to be true.

The same objection cuts at this brief's method. A protocol that scores every claim by whether it can be independently re-derived will rate deterrence as failure and documentation as success, and will therefore under-read the actors moving the world and over-read the ones writing about it. Five days of finding that instruments lack enforcement mechanisms, and not once asking whether the absence is weakness or sufficiency.

The decision it forces. If the objection holds, the op-ed's central claim is not "there is no test" — a smart reader will answer that no sanctions regime has ever had one — but the narrower and more defensible one: the US government's own published measurement of Kimi K3 does not mention distillation, and its own designation process for such models is classified, so no external party can ever check whether the threat was proportionate. That is about auditability, not evidence, and it survives the objection. Write that version.
