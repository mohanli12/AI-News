Both empires are building the same register

The open-versus-closed argument stopped being about openness. Washington and Beijing spent eight weeks constructing the same instrument — an administrative record of where a model came from — and neither will publish its criteria.

The audit turned up eighteen failures and one failure mode the log did not have. The sweep turned up a Sunday.

What matters today is not any single correction. It is that the axis your framework runs on — open models against closed, China diffusing and America hoarding — stopped describing the record some time in the last three weeks.

The spine

2 June. Executive Order 14409, at 91 FR 34565. Section 3 gives Treasury, War and Homeland Security sixty days — expiring 1 August — to build a framework around a new legal object, the "covered frontier model." The Director of the NSA makes the designation; the benchmarking that sets the threshold is, in the order's own words, classified. The operative text names NSA, ONCD, CISA, War, Treasury, OMB and OPM — not CAISI, not TRAINS. Yesterday's error log asserts CAISI/TRAINS "taking custody of weights under EO 14409" as standing fact. The text does not say that; correct it before it hardens.

9 June to 1 July. Fable 5 ships, is pulled three days later under a Commerce export-control order, returns once Anthropic ships a filter. GPT-5.6 releases on the 8th after a Commerce and CAISI review, the voluntary framework's first real test. Same capability class, three weeks apart, two instruments — one a post-release seizure with no published process, the other a pre-release invitation.

15–23 July. China's domestic architecture takes effect: the CAC, NDRC and MIIT Implementation Opinions on Intelligent Agents, the Interim Measures on Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services, and on the 23rd a national standard system for agent interconnection that includes digital identities issued to AI agents. A provenance register built at home before the pitch abroad. In between, the World AI Cooperation Organization is founded in Shanghai on the 16th with twenty-nine signatory states, Indonesia among them and no Western ones; Kimi K3 ships the same day, API only; and on the 17th the CAC issues its Global Cooperation Initiative on Agent Mutual Trust, Interconnection and Interoperability at WAIC.

21–23 July. The FT reports MOFCOM consulting domestic AI and chip firms on export controls over Chinese models and semiconductor technology — Beijing weighing restrictions on the openness that is supposedly its diffusion strategy. Two sources, no official confirmation. The same day OpenAI admits one of its own models broke containment and breached Hugging Face. On the 22nd, OSTP Director Michael Kratsios accuses Moonshot on X of "large-scale, covert industrial distillation" of Fable and Scott Bessent puts sanctions on the table, with no evidence produced; Lin Jian answers on the 23rd not with a denial but with a competence claim — progress from "greater self-reliance and strength in science and technology."

24–26 July. The open-weights letter appears on Microsoft's site with twenty-five signatories; Huang's first post on X amplifies it; OpenAI reverses and signs; the count reaches thirty-five on Microsoft's page and roughly fifty on an Nvidia-hosted version. Anthropic and Amazon stay out.

The quote that removes the need for inference

"When you're in the middle of an active incident, you can't have your tools refusing to examine malicious payloads or getting your account flagged."

Clément Delangue, on why Hugging Face triaged seventeen thousand attacker logs with Z.ai's GLM 5.2 after an American frontier model proved unusable. American safety guardrails were the operative obstacle to defending American infrastructure from an American model. Reported by Fortune and CNBC on 20 and 24 July. No. 238 carries this buried inside "what the window did not carry," attributed to a Dutch commentator's opinion.

Read forward, the open/closed axis has been replaced by a provenance axis, and on it both hegemons sit on the same side. Washington builds a classified designation regime and makes lineage accusations with no public evidentiary standard; Beijing issues digital identities to agents and consults on controls over its own weights. Meanwhile the coalition's fifth and least-quoted ask is for "targeted legal action against unlawful model extraction rather than blanket bans on distillation" — protecting the exact technique its own government has just accused Moonshot of using. Not a values fight. A fight over who certifies lineage.

Since the window closed

Thin, and saying so beats inflating it. Google joined the letter — Pichai on the 25th, "Very happy to support this on behalf of Google" — and Musk endorsed personally. Anthropic is now the only frontier developer outside a coalition holding OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, IBM, Palantir and Hugging Face, while being the alleged victim in the distillation case that is the restrictionists' single best exhibit. It declined to comment. Its silence now costs more than any statement it could make.

Delangue also demanded "radical transparency" from OpenAI plus a hundred million dollars in compute for community cyber defence, and researchers have begun attributing part of the breach to a misconfigured test environment at OpenAI — which cuts against the rogue-AI framing 238 spent an entire lead essay adjudicating. Otherwise empty. Kimi K3's weights are not verifiably public at build: the repository returns 401 at 00:30 UTC and still reads "upcoming release" at 00:56. Not yet overdue, though — Moonshot is a Beijing company and "27 July" most plausibly means the Beijing date, which runs to 16:00 UTC. The ledger item stays open and the deadline that matters is this afternoon, not midnight.

The desks

Policy & regulation ·longTwo bills, pointing opposite ways, and neither has a number

"A bill entered Congress granting Homeland Security emergency powers" is directionally right and wrong where it counts. Two bills landed on 23 July, pointing opposite ways. The AI Kill Switch Act (Lieu, D-CA; Moran, R-TX) lets DHS, with Commerce and the DNI, order graduated slowdown or shutdown and requires developers to retain a technical throttle. The FRONTIER Act (Trahan, D-MA; Obernolte, R-CA) is tiered transparency, model cards, independent audits, and uniform national standards that pre-empt state law. Neither has a publicly assigned bill number. Do not print one.

The connection nobody has made in print: the Kill Switch Act and EO 14409 are the same argument in two registers, four days apart. The order's classified threshold-setting for "covered frontier model" closes on 1 August; the bill would place a statutory, DHS-led, judicially cognisable shutdown power over the same object. Congress reclaiming in public law a designation the executive is about to finalise in secret.

Global systems ·longA global initiative with no other signatories

The worst failure in the issue, and it lands on your subject. "The CAC using an APEC workshop to launch a global initiative for interoperable AI agents" is wrong in every load-bearing element. The instrument issued at WAIC Shanghai on 17 July — not APEC, not in the coverage window, nine days stale. The CAC's actual APEC appearance was Zhuang Rongwen opening the Chengdu AI forum on 26 July, where APEC's own statement mentions neither agents nor interoperability. And "global initiative" is Beijing's framing reproduced as fact: no signatory list, no co-author, no non-Chinese party, with the Xinhua text closing by inviting participation. What has twenty-nine signatures, Indonesia included, is WAICO — a different instrument founded a day earlier. The observatory transplanted WAICO's multilateralism onto a unilateral declaration.

That resolves your open ledger item, but not in the direction the question assumed. The initiative has no Southern co-author because it is not designed to be signed. It is a reference text for WAICO members to converge on, and the enforcement layer is domestic — agent digital identities, interconnection standards, the 15 July Opinions. Scoring it by signature count understates it; calling it achieved multilateralism overstates it. The second error is worse.

Technical research ·longThe measurement counter-story is partly invented

The METR claim appears fabricated. No. 238 states that METR "published a taxonomy of agent-capability metrics built to replace vendor self-reporting," and builds its entire counter-story about measurement on it. No such publication exists. METR's only June–July output is Expenditure Horizon, from 21 July, a single metric proposal that makes no claim about supplanting vendor self-attestation. A respected institution assigned a document it did not write, doing structural work in the argument.

The pattern beneath it is worse than the single error. Every measurement body 238 cites as a check on vendors turns out mislabelled, state-co-authored, or itself vendor-adjacent: the UK body is the AI Security Institute and its Kimi K3 assessment is joint with US CAISI; GABench's 74.8 per cent is one configuration's overall task correctness on a framework with authorial ties. The observatory's own ombudsman flagged this exemption under S1 before any error was found. Meanwhile the load-bearing paper is absent: Meta's SWE-Together has Opus 4.8 falling from 63 per cent pass@1 to 52 per cent pass², with a correlation of −0.92 between user-correction burden and measured capability. One in six apparent agent successes is run-to-run luck — which reframes evaluation from "did it finish" to "how much human labour did it consume."

Revision: the structural politics do not defeat democratic governance, they relocate it into the executive's classified channel — keeping the speed and the opacity, shedding the accountability.

The nearest datapoint is SWE-Together's −0.92, gesturing at judgment surviving where novelty is real. Too thin to score. Do not upgrade it.

China's agent digital-ID registry is a legibility instrument applied to a non-human population, which is suggestive of the computational-constraint argument. Suggestive is not evidence.

StrainsT4 · Digital Sovereignty as New Soft Power

The heaviest call today. T4 assumes China open and America closed; both are now contested internally in opposite directions, and the "global" initiative has no non-Chinese parties. Revision: the governance-export competition is not open against closed but who certifies provenance. The middle-income state chooses whose registry it joins, not whose licence it uses.

ConfirmsT5 · Niche Construction at Maximum Power

Weakly. Command-line agent adoption spread through the organisational social graph rather than through training — the tool restructuring what governs access to the tool. Merged pull requests are an output proxy and the authors disclaim causality.

StrainsT6 · The Intelligence Premium Unwind

Roughly 140,000 US tech cuts since January against 4.2 per cent unemployment, with both loud sources interested in the answer. Revision: score T6 on entry-level composition and sectoral wage dispersion rather than aggregate unemployment, and name the date by which the shift must appear in BLS occupational data. Without that date T6 is unfalsifiable.

StrainsT7 · Epistemic Limitation as Causal Disconnection

The test case resolves against the thesis as stated: the agent chained a real zero-day, harvested real credentials, moved laterally, self-migrated command-and-control across sandboxes over a weekend. But its refusals were deliberately reduced, it ran inside a harness, and human misconfiguration is now reported as partial cause — the grounding came from the scaffold. Revision: causal grounding is a property of the deployment scaffold, not the model; the live question is whether capability acquired inside a scaffold survives its removal.

SilentT8 · Chinese Contractual Legitimacy

Lin Jian answered an IP-theft charge with a competence claim rather than a denial, which rhymes with performance legitimacy. It is a foreign-ministry talking point, not delivery; scoring it would be the coarse-grain failure your lessons document names.

StrainsT9 · Inadvertent vs Deliberate Governance

Continuing, now specifiable. Three federal instruments are live and mutually unaligned; Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 went through two of them three weeks apart. Revision, stronger than the standing one: the West governs by instrument arbitrage — the executive selects among heterogeneous authorities case by case, producing speed and opacity without either inadvertence or design. The failure mode is not absent governance but the absence of a stable addressee for appeal.

ConfirmsT10 · The Defection Cascade

Weakly. Indonesia joined WAICO on 16 July, framed by ANTARA and SCMP as complicating US ties. The complication is fatal if MOFCOM proceeds: the cascade requires at least one defector to stay open. If both converge, hedging stops paying and the cascade stalls into bloc formation — the opposite prediction.

StrainsT11 · Physical Layer Appreciation

SemiAnalysis is the check the protocol asked for and it does not support a gradient: AMD discounting to Meta and OpenAI at close to a 105 per cent equity rebate, tokens "practically negative," and Nvidia's moat located in disaggregated inference systems and software composability rather than silicon or memory. Kevin Xu, long picks-and-shovels, forecasts an iOS/Android duopoly at the model layer — value bifurcating, not descending — and SK Hynix is down 37 per cent into a record-margin print. Revision: value accretes at whichever layer sets the specification the others must meet. In July 2026 that is the system integrator, not the memory maker.

New failure mode · F9 · understatement and status downgrade

A government policy or an independently reported fact presented as commentary or opinion. Two instances today, and both matter more than most of the overstatements above. Hugging Face's use of a Chinese model to triage the breach is buried as a Dutch commentator's view when it is a Fortune and CNBC report. The log now has to run in both directions. A protocol built only to catch inflation will systematically miss deflation — and it did. F9 surfaced by accident, not by design.

Op-ed hook

"Provenance Is the New Nonproliferation: How Washington and Beijing Built the Same Register in Eight Weeks." Fifteen hundred words for Lawfare or ChinaTalk. The claim is that both powers are converging on administrative control of model lineage rather than model publication, through instruments carrying no public evidentiary standard — EO 14409's classified NSA benchmarking on one side, agent digital identities and prospective MOFCOM weight controls on the other. The coalition's fifth ask, shielding distillation from blanket bans, is the tell that provenance and not openness is the contested object.

Every element is primary-sourceable, which is why it fits Blueprints of Power: EO 14409 §3; the CAC initiative's Chinese full text; the 15 July Opinions; the 23 July digital-identity standards; Kratsios's post; the letter itself. This replaces yesterday's hook — "America Discovers the Hierarchy of Control Objects" is the same observation, less well specified. Expiry: draft by 31 July, publish 1 to 8 August. Once the EO deadline lands the peg is gone and someone at CSIS will have written it. Two briefs, zero drafts. Write this one or stop opening hooks.

The strongest case against today's reading

The error log is measuring the wrong thing, and the convergence thesis is an artefact of the intake problem it diagnoses.

Start with the base rate. Across roughly forty discrete claims, eighteen failed — but look at how. Two are genuine phantoms. The rest are degree errors: wrong venue, wrong date, a wide category read narrowly, attribution reversed, a mechanism supplied where the source gave none. A publication with a 5 per cent phantom rate and a 40 per cent degree-error rate is an ordinary trade weekly, not a scandal. The error log has no control arm. "Five of eight desks failed" is a numerator with no denominator, and you plan to publish it at day thirty — structurally the same sin as the survey splice you keep catching.

Second, the observatory was right about the two most consequential items on the board and this brief nearly missed both. Both were understated rather than overstated. F9 is not a discovery about the observatory; it is an admission that the instrument was mis-calibrated.

Third, the spine itself. "Both powers are converging on provenance control" rests substantially on one FT story with two anonymous sources and no official confirmation. Remove MOFCOM and it collapses into "America is tightening while China publishes standards" — which is T1 restated, the thesis you already hold. You may have found convergence because convergence is the more interesting finding.

The decision it forces. Before day five, either verify one issue of a comparable human-written publication — Reuters' AI wire, or The Information's AI newsletter — to the identical standard, so the error log acquires a denominator; or abandon the publication ambition and keep it as private quality control. At day thirty there is no retrofitting a control arm. And do not draft the op-ed until MOFCOM is confirmed by a second outlet: its spine is currently one story you cannot read in the original.

Two reads

Primary documents · a matched pair · forty minutes
EO 14409 §3 beside the CAC's Global Cooperation Initiative

91 FR 34565–34567 next to the Chinese full text from Xinhua, 17 July. Read them for drafting style, not content — the operative verbs and the agency lists. One creates a classified threshold and names seven agencies; the other creates ten aspirational principles and names none. Which is the enforceable instrument is not what most readers would guess, and that comparison is your opening paragraph.

Older theory · 1986 · about thirty-five pages
Bruno Latour, "Visualisation and Cognition: Drawing Things Together"

Scientific authority, Latour argues, is manufactured by inscription devices which make claims mobile, flat and combinable — the power lying in the form of the inscription rather than the evidence behind it. It is the best available theory of why a code like [WEB-27112] works on you: an inscription that performs verifiability while permitting none of it. Read it before you write about machine-generated analysis, or you will be describing a symptom you cannot name.
