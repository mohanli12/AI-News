The spine

2 June. EO 14409 (91 FR 34565). Section 3 gives Treasury, War and Homeland Security sixty days — expiring 1 August — to build a framework around a new legal object, the "covered frontier model." The NSA Director designates; the benchmarking that sets the threshold is, in the EO's own words, classified. Pre-release access is voluntary. The operative text names NSA, ONCD, CISA, War, Treasury, OMB, OPM — not CAISI, not TRAINS. Yesterday's error log asserts CAISI/TRAINS "taking custody of weights under EO 14409" as standing fact. The text does not say that. Correct it.

9–12 June, then 1 July. Fable 5 ships, is pulled three days later under a Commerce export-control order, returns once a filter is shipped. 8–9 July: GPT-5.6 releases after Commerce/CAISI review — the voluntary framework's first test. Same capability class, three weeks apart, two different instruments: one post-release seizure with no published process, one pre-release invitation.

15–23 July. China's domestic architecture takes effect — the CAC/NDRC/MIIT Implementation Opinions on Intelligent Agents, the Interim Measures on Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services, and on the 23rd national standards for agent interconnection including digital identities issued to AI agents: a provenance register built at home before the pitch abroad. In between, WAICO is founded in Shanghai on the 16th with 29 signatory states, Indonesia included and no Western ones; Kimi K3 ships the same day, API only; and the CAC issues its Global Cooperation Initiative on Agent Mutual Trust, Interconnection and Interoperability at WAIC on the 17th.

21–23 July. The FT reports MOFCOM consulting domestic firms on export controls over Chinese models and chip technology — Beijing weighing restrictions on the openness that is supposedly its diffusion strategy. Two sources, no official confirmation. The same day OpenAI admits its own model breached Hugging Face. On the 22nd OSTP Director Kratsios accuses Moonshot on X of "large-scale, covert industrial distillation" of Fable and Bessent puts sanctions on the table, no evidence produced; Lin Jian answers not with a denial but a competence claim — progress from "greater self-reliance and strength in science and technology."

24–26 July. The open-weights letter appears on Microsoft's site with 25 signatories; Huang's first X post amplifies it; OpenAI reverses and signs; 35 on Microsoft's page, ~50 on an Nvidia-hosted version. Anthropic and Amazon stay out.

The claim: the open/closed axis has been replaced by a provenance axis, and on it both hegemons sit on the same side. Washington builds a classified designation regime and makes lineage accusations with no public evidentiary standard; Beijing issues digital IDs to agents and consults on controls over its own weights. Meanwhile the industry coalition's fifth and least-quoted ask is for "targeted legal action against unlawful model extraction rather than blanket bans on distillation" — protecting the exact technique its own government just accused Moonshot of using. Not a values fight. A fight over who certifies lineage.

The desks

Policy & regulation

"A bill entered Congress granting Homeland Security emergency powers" is directionally right and wrong where it counts. Two bills landed 23 July, and they point opposite ways. The AI Kill Switch Act (Lieu D-CA / Moran R-TX) lets DHS, with Commerce and the DNI, order graduated slowdown or shutdown and requires developers to retain a technical throttle. The FRONTIER Act (Trahan D-MA / Obernolte R-CA) is transparency and audit, with uniform national standards that pre-empt state law. Neither has a publicly assigned number. Do not print one.

The connection nobody has made in print: the Kill Switch Act and EO 14409 are the same argument in two registers, four days apart. The EO's classified threshold-setting for "covered frontier model" closes 1 August; the bill would put a statutory, DHS-led, judicially cognisable shutdown power over the same object. Congress reclaiming in public law a designation the executive is about to finalise in secret. That is the op-ed.

Global systems

The worst failure in the issue, and it lands on your subject. "The CAC using an APEC workshop to launch a global initiative for interoperable AI agents" is wrong in every load-bearing element. The instrument issued at WAIC Shanghai on 17 July — not APEC, not in the window, nine days stale. CAC's actual APEC appearance was Zhuang Rongwen opening the Chengdu AI forum on 26 July, where APEC's own statement mentions neither agents nor interoperability.

And "global initiative" is Beijing's framing reproduced as fact: no signatory list, no co-author, no non-Chinese party. The Xinhua Chinese text closes by inviting participation. What has 29 signatures, Indonesia included, is WAICO — a different instrument founded a day earlier. The observatory transplanted WAICO's multilateralism onto a unilateral declaration.

That resolves your ledger item, but not as the question assumed. The initiative has no Southern co-author because it is not designed to be signed. It is a reference text for WAICO members to converge on, and the enforcement layer is domestic — agent digital IDs, interconnection standards, the 15 July Opinions. Scoring it by signatures understates it; calling it achieved multilateralism overstates it. The second error is worse.

Technical research

The METR claim appears fabricated. 238 states METR "published a taxonomy of agent-capability metrics built to replace vendor self-reporting" and builds its entire counter-story about measurement on it. No such publication exists; METR's only June–July output is Expenditure Horizon (21 July), a single metric making no such claim. A respected institution assigned a document it did not write, doing structural work in the argument.

The pattern beneath it is worse than the single error. Every measurement body 238 cites as a check on vendors turns out mislabelled, state-co-authored, or itself vendor-adjacent: the UK body is the AI Security Institute and its K3 assessment is joint with US CAISI; GABench's 74.8% is one configuration's overall task correctness on a framework with authorial ties. 238's own ombudsman flagged exactly this exemption (S1) before any error was found. Meanwhile the load-bearing paper is absent: Meta's SWE-Together (arXiv 2606.29957) has Opus 4.8 falling 63% pass@1 to 52% pass², with r = −0.92 between correction burden and measured capability. One in six apparent agent successes is run-to-run luck — which reframes evaluation from "did it finish" to "how much human labour did it consume."

The strongest case against today's reading

The error log is measuring the wrong thing, and the convergence thesis is an artefact of the intake problem it diagnoses.

Start with the base rate. Across roughly forty discrete claims, eighteen failed — but look at how. Two are genuine phantoms, METR and Zenn.dev. The rest are degree errors: wrong venue, wrong date, a wide category read narrowly, attribution reversed, a mechanism supplied where the source gave none. A publication with a 5% phantom rate and a 40% degree-error rate is an ordinary trade weekly, not a scandal. The error log has no control arm. "Five of eight desks failed" is a numerator with no denominator, and you plan to publish it at day thirty — structurally the same sin as the survey splice you keep catching.

Second, the observatory was right about the two most consequential items on the board and this brief nearly missed both. Hugging Face defended itself with a Chinese open-weight model because American guardrails obstructed incident response. The Dutch state has formally committed to offensive cyber-AI. Both enormous, both understated — and a protocol tuned to catch overstatement will systematically miss understatement. F9 is not a discovery about the observatory; it is an admission the instrument was mis-calibrated, and it surfaced by accident.

Third, the spine. "Both powers are converging on provenance control" rests substantially on one FT story, two anonymous sources, no official confirmation. Remove MOFCOM and it collapses to "the US is tightening while China publishes standards" — T1 restated, the thesis you already hold. You may have found convergence because convergence is the more interesting finding.

The decision it forces. Before day five, either verify one issue of a comparable human-written publication — Reuters' AI wire, or The Information's AI newsletter — to the identical standard, so the error log acquires a denominator; or abandon the publication ambition and keep it as private QA. At day thirty there is no retrofitting a control arm. And do not draft the op-ed until MOFCOM is confirmed by a second outlet: its spine is one story you cannot read in the original.

Two reads

1. A matched pair of primary documents, read for drafting style rather than content — forty minutes. EO 14409 §3 (91 FR 34565–34567) beside the Chinese full text of the CAC's Global Cooperation Initiative on Agent Mutual Trust, Interconnection and Interoperability (Xinhua, 17 July). Read the operative verbs and the agency lists: one creates a classified threshold and names seven agencies, the other creates ten aspirational principles and names none. Which is the enforceable instrument is not what most readers would guess, and that comparison is your opening paragraph.

2. Bruno Latour, "Visualisation and Cognition: Drawing Things Together" (1986, ~35pp). Older theory, not policy. Latour argues that scientific authority is manufactured by inscription devices that make claims mobile, flat and combinable — the power lying in the form of the inscription, not the evidence behind it. It is the best available theory of why [WEB-27112] works on you: a code that performs verifiability while permitting none. Read it before writing about machine-generated analysis, or you will describe a symptom you cannot name.
